# Affordable Housing Nonprofit — Back Office & Last-Mile Interactive Graphic

## Reframed premise

The outward-facing story is only half the sale. What actually differentiates Max Power Platform is how the **outside work** of an affordable housing nonprofit — classes, counseling sessions, construction sites, closings, donor events, grant deadlines — automatically becomes clean, auditable, fundable back-office work.

The interactive graphic should show three connected lanes:

1. **Outside work** — what staff and clients do in the real world.
2. **Back-office handling** — the data, documents, approvals, and workflows that must happen for every outside touch.
3. **Last mile** — the compliance, reporting, payment, and audit-trail closure that keeps the lights on and funders happy.

Every outside activity has a back-office cost. Max Power Suite is the system that pays that cost automatically.

## The "last mile" is where most systems fail

Most nonprofit software handles the first 80% of any workflow. The expensive, risky part is the last 20%:

- The 9902 report that proves counseling hours.
- The lien that secures the down payment loan.
- The draw package that releases construction funds.
- The recertification that keeps LIHTC compliance.
- The receipt that keeps a donor tax-deductible.
- The audit trail that survives a funder monitoring visit.
- The board report that justifies next year's budget.

The graphic must make the last mile visible. It is not a footnote; it is the product.

## Interactive graphic concepts for back-office + last mile

### Concept A: Three-lane "conveyor belt"

A horizontal diagram with three stacked lanes: **Outside Work** (top), **Back Office** (middle), **Last Mile** (bottom). A vertical "case line" moves left-to-right through the lanes as the user scrolls or clicks Next.

- At each step, the case line highlights the outside activity and the back-office tasks it creates.
- A counter shows how many documents, approvals, and reports are auto-generated.
- A "system badge" appears showing which Max Power module is doing the work.
- At the end, the case line hits a "Compliance & Audit Ready" gate.

**Best for:** A hero section that tells the full story in one scroll.

### Concept B: "Inside the office" dashboard wall

Show the back office as a literal wall of work: stacks of paper, spreadsheets, sticky notes, and emails on the left. As the user clicks each outside-work activity, the paper turns into a digital record on the right and flies into the correct module bucket.

- Start with a cluttered desk metaphor.
- Click "Class held" → roster becomes a digital attendance record → certificate generates → 9902 report updates.
- Click "Draw requested" → invoice, lien release, inspection, and budget check all resolve → approved → paid.
- End with a clean dashboard and a green "Audit Ready" stamp.

**Best for:** Prospects who live with paper chaos and need to feel the pain of the current state.

### Concept C: Role-based "last mile" checklist

Pick a role (Finance Director, Compliance Manager, Executive Director, Program Director). Show that person's quarterly or annual checklist and how each item is auto-completed by the suite.

- Finance Director: 9902 export, rent roll, AP close, donor receipts, grant drawdowns.
- Compliance Manager: certificate audit, recertification reminders, lien tracking, BABA flags.
- Executive Director: board KPI deck, funder reports, outcome metrics, budget variance.

**Best for:** Selling to the people who sign the checks and answer to the board.

### Concept D: "No dead ends" network graph

An SVG network where every outside-work node is connected to the back-office records and last-mile outputs it produces. The visual promise is that nothing falls into a spreadsheet gap.

- Nodes: intake, class, counseling, application, closing, construction draw, lease, rent, maintenance, donation, grant report, audit.
- Edges: data flows (contact, property, invoice, payment, certificate, report).
- Hover an edge → show the exact record and module.
- Highlight dead-end paths in red (current state) and connected paths in green (with Max Power).

**Best for:** Technical buyers and implementation partners who need to see integration architecture.

### Concept E: "A week in the life" — back-office heat map

A calendar heat map showing the back-office workload that a typical nonprofit faces in a month: 9902 deadline, board meeting, rent recertifications, grant report, construction draw, payroll, AP close. Show how many hours each takes without Max Power, and how many with Max Power.

- Click a deadline → expand the tasks and the modules that handle them.
- Show a "before/after" hours estimate.
- Include a quote or stat about audit risk or staff burnout.

**Best for:** ROI conversations with executive directors and finance.

## Recommended first build: Concept A + Concept C hybrid

Create a `/last-mile/` page with two sections:

1. **The three-lane conveyor belt** (Concept A) — shows the outside-work → back-office → last-mile flow for one complete example: a first-time homebuyer from intake through closing.
2. **Role-based last-mile checklists** (Concept C) — three tabs: Finance Director, Compliance Manager, Executive Director. Each tab shows what they must produce and how the suite auto-generates it.

This keeps the page focused on the operational pain and the operational solution, not just feature lists.

## What the graphic must prove

1. **No re-entry.** Every outside activity creates a digital record once, and every downstream report pulls from that same record.
2. **Compliance is built in.** 9902, CAPER, LIHTC, HUD, BABA, Davis-Bacon — the outputs are generated as a byproduct of doing the work.
3. **The last mile is automatic.** Certificates, liens, draws, receipts, recertifications, and board reports are not afterthoughts.
4. **One vendor runs it all.** Managed M365 provides identity, security, email, and AI; the Max Power Suite handles the mission-specific operations.

---

## Copy principles for the back-office / last-mile graphic

- **Lead with the pain, not the product.** "Where does the paper go after the class?" is a better opener than "Our HBE module has attendance tracking."
- **Name the outside activity first.** Then show the back-office cost. Then show the closure.
- **Use real verbs:** route, verify, approve, disburse, record, release, submit, reconcile.
- **Quantify the last mile:** "One class = 30 attendance records + 30 certificates + 1 9902 report."
- **Avoid feature lists.** The graphic is about workflow, not checkboxes.

## Technical implementation notes

- Build as a custom Power Pages web template with inline SVG and vanilla JS.
- Keep the animation lightweight; no external animation libraries.
- Use CSS transitions for the conveyor belt and tab switching.
- Make the diagram keyboard-accessible and screen-reader friendly with ARIA labels.
- Generate the content from the same module metadata in `scripts/Build-Site.ps1` so the page stays in sync with the rest of the site.
